= Try it: a load, with Kafka away
:description: S7's first measurements, the same five users and the same minute without Kafka, on the services and on the modulith.

xref:runtime::cluster-guide.adoc#load[The load script] runs the walkthrough against the edge: each virtual user plans a shipment, picks it up, departs, delivers it to the Hadimkoy hub, then reads the warehouses and a road distance. A second terminal stops Kafka while that runs. k6 says what the caller saw. The drill says what was waiting behind the edge.

The pair was run once on each side. The services, on 2026-11-21. The modulith, on 2026-11-22, after the switch below. Five virtual users, 30 seconds of warm-up, four minutes measured, Kafka away for 60 seconds. Startup time is not in these files.

[[run]]
== How a run is started

The two commands are the same whichever side is active: the edge answers for it. They have to start against a side that is already healthy. During a switch nothing answers on port 8081, and the users would time that gap.

The services run was already on that side, so it began at the two commands. The modulith run began with the switch (xref:switching.adoc#back[the same steps, the other way]), from WSL:

[source,bash]
----
cd /mnt/d/dev/workspace/ff-logistic/runtime
sed -i 's/^active: .*/active: modulith/' deploy/implementation.yaml
git diff                      # -active: services  +active: modulith
git commit -am "Switch to the modulith" && git push
kubectl -n argocd annotate applicationset ff-implementation argocd.argoproj.io/application-set-refresh=true --overwrite
until argocd app get logistics-app > /dev/null 2>&1; do sleep 5; done
argocd app sync logistics-app
argocd app wait logistics-app --health --timeout 300
argocd app sync edge
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" localhost:8081/actuator/info
----

`200` is the modulith. The ApplicationSet deletes the four service Applications by itself, and their pods with them. `logistics-app` then exists as `Missing` and `OutOfSync`: generated, not synced. The sync runs the migration Job, starts the Rollout at 100 %, and runs the application's own smoke test. The edge is last. Its smoke test checks who answers, and it fails if it runs while nobody is behind it.

An expired `argocd` session answers `Unauthenticated` and syncs nothing. Ctrl+C stops the terminal, not a sync Argo CD has already accepted. `argocd relogin`, then `argocd app get logistics-app`: if it is already `Healthy`, do not sync it again. Sync the edge, and read the HTTP code.

Then two terminals, both in `runtime`. The drill starts once the load is printing:

[source,bash]
----
deploy/load/run.sh 5 4m 30          # writes load-<side>-<time>.txt
----

[source,bash]
----
deploy/load/drill.sh kafka 60       # writes drill-kafka-<side>-<time>.txt
----

The load samples CPU and memory at rest, runs the users, then samples again. The drill stops the broker for a minute and, every few seconds, counts deliveries, arrivals, analytics rows and events still in the outbox, until those have caught up. On the services the outbox is `transportation.event_publication`. On the modulith it is `modulith.event_publication`. Flow 2 opens the arrival in-process on the modulith, and through Kafka on the services. Analytics crosses Kafka on both.

[[services]]
== The services, 2026-11-21

`deploy/load/run.sh 5 4m 30` started at 08:04:24Z. Twelve seconds later the drill stopped the broker.

[[caller]]
=== What the caller saw

Warm-up is not counted. Of the four minutes that are, Kafka was down for about the first minute (it was scaled back at 08:05:40Z) and up for the rest.

[source]
----
side: services  vus: 5  warm-up: 30s  measured: 4m
iterations: 1125  (4.69/s)  requests: 7880  (32.8/s)  failed: 0.00%
request      count  failed  med ms  p95 ms  p99 ms  max ms
sign-in          5    0.0%    66.8    70.6    71.3    71.4
plan          1125    0.0%    20.1    44.5    86.2   134.8
shipment      1125    0.0%     3.8    13.4    17.4    22.0
pickup        1125    0.0%    11.9    20.2    27.0    34.3
departure     1125    0.0%    10.2    17.2    21.8    29.8
deliver       1125    0.0%     9.7    16.6    20.1    26.5
warehouses    1125    0.0%     4.0     7.6    10.1    13.9
distance      1125    0.0%     2.3     4.1     6.2    16.6
----

Nothing failed, including the deliveries made while the broker was down. A delivery answers from the database commit; the event leaves afterwards (xref:try-failures.adoc#kafka-down[the same thing, one shipment at a time]). The slowest delivery in the four minutes was 26.5 ms.

`plan` is the slow request. It is flow 1: the shipment is stored, and transportation asks routing for the road distance over HTTP. A direct `distance` call, the same two points and no shipment, is the fastest line in the table. The three writes sit together, around 10 ms at the median.

Five users finished 4.69 iterations a second. Each iteration then waits one second, as a person would, so the pause sets the rate. This run is what a small desk looks like, not how far the side can be pushed.

Sign-in happened five times, once per user, at the start of the run. A token lasts five minutes and the measured window is four, so it stays off the hot path.

[[pods]]
=== What the pods cost

`kubectl top`, sampled about every 15 seconds. At rest, before the users started; under load, the highest sample of each pod. CPU is millicores.

[cols="3,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| | At rest, CPU | At rest, MiB | Under load, CPU | Under load, MiB

| gateway (1 pod) | 1 | 16 | 21 | 19
| routing (2) | 5 | 733 | 85 | 746
| transportation (2) | 12 | 897 | 379 | 932
| warehousing (2) | 9 | 767 | 113 | 786
| *the services side* | *27* | *2413* | *598* | *2483*
|===

Memory barely moved: 70 MiB on 2.4 GiB. The JVMs were already resident at rest, which is most of what seven pods cost when nobody is calling. CPU did move, and transportation took most of it (379 of 598). It is the service every iteration writes three times, and the one that asks routing.

The platform both sides share, over the same window:

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| | CPU, millicores | MiB

| Kafka | 584 | 415
| PostgreSQL | 86 | 199
| analytics loader | 26 | 289
| identity | 6 | 270
| *shared platform* | *702* | *1173*
|===

At this rate the broker used about as much CPU as the seven service pods together. Identity barely shows: the applications check tokens locally, and five sign-ins are the whole of its work (xref:architecture.adoc#layers[the identity rule]).

[[kafka]]
=== Kafka away for a minute

The drill counts from its own start (08:04:36Z). `delivered` is shipments delivered, `arrivals` is flow 2, `analytics` is rows the loader stored, `outbox` is events in `transportation.event_publication` not yet handed to Kafka. The four numbers of one line are four queries, one after another, so while traffic is moving the later columns are a few seconds newer. A healthy line can show analytics a handful ahead of delivered for that reason alone.

Kafka was still up on the first line. From the next line the arrivals and the analytics figure freeze, and the outbox grows by about three rows per delivery: pickup, departure and the delivery each publish one event (`ShipmentProgressed` twice, then `ShipmentDelivered`).

[source]
----
  time  kafka     delivered  arrivals analytics  outbox
   +2s  up                5         7        10       0
   +8s  away             40        15        15      75
  +63s  away            295        15        15     855
-- kafka back at +64s
  +80s  back            370        15        15    1095
  +87s  back            405       155       160     760
 +271s  back           1225      1085      1085     410
 +476s  back           1225      1225      1225       0
-- caught up 412s after kafka came back
----

Scaling the broker back is not the moment it accepts publishes. From +64s to +80s the outbox was still climbing, and arrivals stayed at 15. At +87s both consumers were reading again: arrivals and analytics jumped by about 140, and the outbox dropped by 335.

k6 stopped at about +271s (delivered stays 1225 from then on). The outbox still held 410 events, and it stepped down by 50 to 100 rows roughly every half minute, which is how often the upkeep looks for rows that are due. The last of them cleared at +476s. Caught up means arrivals and analytics within three of the deliveries, and no more than three events left in the outbox: what is in flight at one moment of this load.

1225 delivered, 1225 arrivals, 1225 analytics, an empty outbox. Nothing was lost, and nobody replayed anything by hand. The caller, from the table above, did not see the minute.

The earlier drill, one shipment and Kafka down for several minutes, waited about seven minutes because of the doubling pause between tries (xref:try-failures.adoc#timeline[that timeline]). Here the outage was a minute. The 412 seconds are a backlog of about a thousand events, emptied on that same pause, while new deliveries kept arriving until the users stopped.

=== Kafka away for a minute

The outbox column is `modulith.event_publication`. Arrivals did not stop. Analytics did.

[source]
----
  time  kafka     delivered  arrivals analytics  outbox
   +1s  up                2         5         5       0
   +8s  away             32        35         7      84
  +62s  away            285       285         7     834
-- kafka back at +62s
  +81s  back            375       375         7     700
  +87s  back            405       405       171     700
 +258s  back           1200      1200      1132     200
 +293s  back           1200      1200      1200       0
-- caught up 231s after kafka came back
----

From the moment the broker was gone, arrivals stayed with the deliveries: flow 2 is a call in the same process, after the commit. Analytics froze at 7 until +87 s, about twenty-five seconds after the scale-up, then started reading. The outbox grew by about three rows per delivery, the same two `ShipmentProgressed` events and one `ShipmentDelivered`, and stepped down once the broker accepted them. k6 stopped at about +258 s, on 1200 deliveries. The last analytics rows and the last outbox rows cleared together at +293 s.

1200 delivered, 1200 arrivals, 1200 analytics, an empty outbox. The caller, from the table above, did not see the minute. The dock did not wait for it either. Analytics did, for just under four minutes after the broker came back. On the services the dock waited too, and the same backlog took 412 seconds.

* *Flow 1 is the request that moves.* Booking a road leg asks routing. A method call against an HTTP call is the whole of the difference in `plan`. The writes, and the direct distance call, are already short on both sides.
* *Flow 2 is where a minute without Kafka is felt.* On the services the dock learns late. On the modulith the dock learns in-process, and only analytics, which crosses Kafka on both sides, waits.
* *Memory is paid at rest, and the modulith pays for fewer processes.* Load added CPU on both. The services added almost no memory. The modulith added some, and stayed the smaller set.
* *These are two runs of five users, one minute of Kafka, not a capacity test.* The one-second pause sets the rate. Startup time, a database that is away, and a stopped area are not in these files.
