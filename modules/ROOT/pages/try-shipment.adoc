= Try it: a shipment to a warehouse, step by step
:description: The S4 walkthrough as numbered steps: a shipment delivered to a warehouse opens an inbound arrival there, in-process on the modulith, over Kafka on the services.

Flow 2 of xref:architecture.adoc#flow-2[the architecture]: a shipment is booked to the warehouse of xref:try-warehousing.adoc[Try it: a warehouse] (`IST-HDK`, GLN `8690001000011`), travels, and is delivered; the delivery opens an *inbound arrival* at that warehouse's dock. First on the modulith, then on the services, against the same edge.

Run one block at a time and compare its answer with the ✅ line. The answers shown are from the first run, on 2026-11-20; ids, times and the shipment numbers differ in yours (the numbers count on with every shipment ever booked: `SHP-10001` only on the very first).

Everything runs in one WSL terminal; the later steps use the variables the earlier ones set.

[[before]]
== Before: the release and the schemas

The S4 images are rolled out (`argocd app sync logistics-app`, xref:walkthrough.adoc#s4[walkthrough]).

=== 1. The new tables, and the modulith's outbox

[source,bash]
----
PSQL="kubectl -n ff-logistics exec deploy/postgres -- psql -U fflogistics -d ff_logistics -c"
$PSQL "select 'transportation' area, version, description from transportation_flyway_history
       union all select 'warehousing', version, description from warehousing_flyway_history where version::int >= 3
       union all select 'modulith outbox', version, description from modulith.outbox_flyway_history
       order by 1, 2"
----

✅ Transportation at version 2 (its tables), warehousing at 4 (the inbound arrivals), and a new schema `modulith` with its own outbox history:

----

-----------------+---------+------------------------------

----

[[modulith]]
== On the modulith

=== 3. Book a shipment to the warehouse

From Cologne to the Istanbul hub: by road to the rail terminal, by train to Trieste, by Ro-Ro ferry to Istanbul, the last 60 km by road. `destinationGln` names the warehouse.

[source,bash]
----
SHP=$(curl -s "${H[@]}" $E/api/v1/tms/shipments -d '{"consignorName":"Rhein Parts GmbH",
  "consigneeName":"Istanbul Hadimkoy Hub","destinationGln":"8690001000011",
  "equipmentCode":"CSQU3054383","sscc":"106141411234567897","incoterm":"DAP",
  "origin":"Cologne Eifeltor","destination":"Istanbul Hadimkoy","grossWeightKg":18000,
  "legs":[
    {"mode":"ROAD","origin":"Cologne Eifeltor","destination":"Cologne Eifeltor Terminal","distanceKm":15,"carrierReference":"DE-K-FF-210"},
    {"mode":"RAIL","origin":"Cologne Eifeltor Terminal","destination":"Trieste","distanceKm":1050,"carrierReference":"TXL-41877"},
    {"mode":"SEA_RORO","origin":"Trieste","destination":"Istanbul Pendik","distanceKm":1450,"carrierReference":"DFDS-PT-2611"},
    {"mode":"ROAD","origin":"Istanbul Pendik","destination":"Istanbul Hadimkoy","distanceKm":60,"carrierReference":"TR-34-FF-0417"}]}')
echo "$SHP" | jq '{shipmentNumber, ecmrNumber, status, destinationGln, footprint}'
ID=$(echo "$SHP" | jq -r .id)
----

✅ The first shipment of a fresh sequence, planned, with its footprint:

----
{
  "shipmentNumber": "SHP-10001",
  "ecmrNumber": "ECMR-10001",
  "status": "PLANNED",
  "destinationGln": "8690001000011",
  "footprint": {
    "totalDistanceKm": 2575.0,
    "co2eKg": 1308.6,
    "roadOnlyCo2eKg": 2873.7,
    "savedCo2eKg": 1565.1,
    "reductionPercent": 54.46
  }
}
----

On the first run the number was `SHP-10003`: two shipments had been booked before. The footprint is the same for every run of this plan.

The footprint (EN 16258 / ISO 14083 factors per transport mode): 18 t over 2,575 km by rail and sea emits 1.3 t CO2e, less than half of what the same trip by road alone would.

[source,bash]

curl -s "${H[@]}" $E/api/v1/tms/shipments/$ID/delivery -d '{"receivedBy":"Dock 2, A. Yilmaz"}' | jq '{status, deliveredAt}'
curl -s "${H[@]}" $E/api/v1/wms/arrivals | jq '.[0] | {shipmentNumber, sscc, grossWeightKg, status}'

✅ The shipment `DELIVERED`, and an arrival open at the dock, for the same shipment:

{

{
  "shipmentNumber": "SHP-10003",

Nobody called warehousing: transportation published `ShipmentDelivered`, the `flows` module heard it after the commit and opened the arrival. An empty list means the second call was quicker than the listener; ask again.

== On the services

=== 7. Switch

Everything until now ran on the modulith. Move the lever (xref:switching.adoc[Switching sides]), one line at a time:

[source,bash]
----
cd /mnt/d/dev/workspace/ff-logistic/runtime
git pull
sed -i 's/^active: .*/active: services/' deploy/implementation.yaml
git commit -am "Switch to the services" && git push
kubectl -n argocd annotate applicationset ff-implementation argocd.argoproj.io/application-set-refresh=true --overwrite
until argocd app get warehousing > /dev/null 2>&1; do sleep 5; done
argocd app sync -l ff-logistic/implementation=services
argocd app wait -l ff-logistic/implementation=services --health --timeout 300
argocd app sync edge
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" localhost:8081/actuator/info
----

✅ The last line answers `404`: the gateway answers now (the modulith has `/actuator/info`, the gateway does not). === 8. The same, through the services

The token has expired by now: step 2's `TOKEN=` and `H=` again. Then a second shipment, the same plan with another order of the day:

[source,bash]

=== 9. What travelled over Kafka

[source,bash]
----
kubectl -n ff-logistics exec kafka-0 -- /opt/kafka/bin/kafka-console-consumer.sh \
  --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --topic ff.logistics.tms.shipment-delivered \
  --from-beginning --max-messages 1 --formatter-property print.key=true --timeout-ms 10000
echo $ID2
----

✅ One message: the key on the left is the shipment's id (the same as `$ID2`), the value transportation's `ShipmentDelivered` as JSON:

----
cd9c64e0-5fda-4bf7-8133-3231f23bbb52    {"shipmentId":"cd9c64e0-5fda-4bf7-8133-3231f23bbb52","tenantId":"ff-eu-freight",
"shipmentNumber":"SHP-10005","customerOrderReference":null,"totalDistanceKm":2575.0,"savedCo2eKg":1565.1,
"deliveredAt":"2026-11-20T12:46:25.995991485Z","destinationGln":"8690001000011","sscc":"106141411234567897",
"grossWeightKg":18000.0}
Processed a total of 1 messages
----

The first on the topic, although four deliveries came before it: the modulith's never left its JVM.

`Processed a total of 0 messages` after a `TimeoutException` means the topic is empty: the broker answered, there was nothing to read. Here that means the delivery did not go through the services (step 7).

[source,bash]
----
kubectl -n ff-logistics exec kafka-0 -- /opt/kafka/bin/kafka-consumer-groups.sh \
  --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --group warehousing
----

✅ The group `warehousing`: partition 0 holds the one message (`LOG-END-OFFSET` 1), read (`CURRENT-OFFSET` 1, `LAG` 0). The topic's three partitions are shared out between the service's two pods, one consumer each:

----
GROUP        TOPIC                               PARTITION  CURRENT-OFFSET  LOG-END-OFFSET  LAG  HOST
warehousing  ff.logistics.tms.shipment-delivered 0          1               1               0    /10.42.0.143
warehousing  ff.logistics.tms.shipment-delivered 1          -               0               -    /10.42.0.143
warehousing  ff.logistics.tms.shipment-delivered 2          -               0               -    /10.42.0.144
----

Within a group, each partition goes to one pod; a message to one of them. Which partition a message lands on follows from its key, the shipment: all messages about one shipment stay in order on one partition.

=== 11. Two arrivals, one from each side

[source,bash]
----
$PSQL "select a.shipment_number, a.status, a.created_at, w.code
       from wms_inbound_arrivals a join wms_warehouses w on w.id = a.warehouse_id order by a.created_at"
----

✅ Every arrival at `IST-HDK`, the services' last:

----
 shipment_number | status |          created_at           |  code
-----------------+--------+-------------------------------+---------
 SHP-10001       | OPEN   | 2026-11-20 12:11:06.456188+00 | IST-HDK
 SHP-10002       | OPEN   | 2026-11-20 12:12:40.586781+00 | IST-HDK
 SHP-10003       | OPEN   | 2026-11-20 12:36:40.43213+00  | IST-HDK
 SHP-10004       | OPEN   | 2026-11-20 12:38:31.955114+00 | IST-HDK
 SHP-10005       | OPEN   | 2026-11-20 12:46:26.505225+00 | IST-HDK

The rows do not say which side opened them: the same core opened all of them, four times called by a listener in the same JVM, once by a Kafka consumer in another process.

[[window]]
=== The window, measured

The delivery's time (step 5 and step 8 answer with `deliveredAt`) against the arrival's `created_at`:

[cols="2,2,2,1",options="header"]
|===
| | Delivery committed | Arrival opened | Window

| Modulith, `SHP-10003` | 12:36:40.204 | 12:36:40.432 | ≈ 0.23 s
| Services, `SHP-10005` | 12:46:25.996 | 12:46:26.505 | ≈ 0.51 s
|===

Both eventually consistent, both well under a second on an idle laptop: the modulith hands the event to another thread in its JVM; the services send it through the outbox to Kafka, and another process reads it. What the numbers do not show is what can stand in between: on the services side, a broker and another service that may be down. That is the next exercise: xref:try-failures.adoc[Try it: failure drills].
